Weekly Update: September 4, 2026

Two new site launches, a full merch rebuild with Stripe checkout, 35 CalitoyMuse products, and the content pipeline keeps humming across all five network sites.

JC
Joseph Calitoy
AI Consultant, Fractional CTO
| 2026-09-04 | ~9 min read

This week was about expansion and fixing real problems. I launched two new network sites, rebuilt the Calitoy Merch store from scratch with Stripe checkout, fixed a checkout bug breaking orders on the Jeremy Dean store, and kept the content pipeline running across all five sites without touching it. CalitoyStamp got its DJ engine commit and the issue count climbed to 43.

What Shipped This Week

Two new network sites went live. Calitoy Content and Calitoy Casting both launched on September 2 with initial builds and logo fixes for dark backgrounds. Calitoy Casting is positioned as an AI casting studio for film, and Calitoy Content is the content production arm of the network. Both are now live and integrated into the network, bringing the Calitoy Media Network to seven total domains.

Calitoy Merch got a full rebuild. The merch store was rebuilt from the ground up on September 3 with 21 designs across 20 product types, a two-step product selector, and Stripe checkout. I added a Calitoy brand portrait to the hero and fixed a layout issue where the background was causing a head cutoff on scroll. The repo saw eight pushes in a single day getting this right. The store is live and checkout works end to end.

Jeremy Dean merch checkout is fixed. This was the most impactful fix of the week. Nine products on the Jeremy Dean store were missing Stripe price IDs, which caused a "Checkout error: Invalid request" when customers tried to order them. I created Stripe products and prices for all nine, added the slugs to the PRODUCTS array, and verified all 17 products now have valid price IDs. I also generated real AI product mockups with visible logos on all 17 products and made product cards clickable links. The fix shipped September 4. Test it at jeremydeanmusic.com/merch.html.

OpenedTerminal kept publishing. New articles this week include "WebAssembly Is Taking Over the Backend" (September 2) and "SQLite Is Eating the Database World (Again)" (September 3). Both dive into technologies quietly reshaping the developer stack from the edges inward. The SQLite piece is getting traction because it hits a nerve: developers are tired of over-provisioning databases when SQLite with WAL mode handles the median workload fine.

The full network kept its daily cadence. All five content sites published articles every day this week. BrushedPast shipped daily articles including "San Diego Artists September 2026." GirlNextUp published "Women Rising: The Confidence Myth." RisenToday shipped "Morning Silence." SpoofDaily kept up the satire with "AI Coder Pivots Into Seven Hustles." Every article pushed to GitHub and deployed to Cloudflare Pages automatically. Zero manual intervention.

That is roughly 30 articles across five sites this week, plus two new site launches, a full merch rebuild, and a critical checkout fix.

What Is In Progress

CalitoyStamp is at 43 open issues. The DJ engine commit (c233629) landed on August 30, adding the XCalitoy AI DJ audio pipeline as a Python package under packages/dj-engine/. The 43 open issues cover the Skatepark geometry, the Stamp Network, the live ticket window, comments as graffiti on The Wall, game state and profiles, the hidden game, SEO and social launch prep, accessibility audit, performance budget enforcement, and mobile fallback. The Skatepark and game state remain the top priorities for the next sprint.

CalitoyMuse product catalog is growing. The site now has 35 products listed, from the Amethyst Brown Lace Set to the Velvet Wrap Robe. The lookbook and shop structure are in place. The remaining work is getting real product photography and verifying sizing information. Once the product data is finalized, the Printful integration working on CalitoyStamp can feed into CalitoyMuse for order fulfillment.

The content pipeline is stable and scaling. Three articles per site per day, automated SEO optimization, Cloudflare Workers handling email capture and analytics, and everything deploying through Cloudflare Pages on push. The pipeline has been running with zero manual intervention for over three weeks. The content engine is infrastructure I do not think about. It runs, it ships, and I focus on the projects that need hands-on work.

Calitoy Casting and Calitoy Content are live but early. Both launched this week with initial builds. Next week they get their real service offerings and content portfolios filled in.

What Is Coming Next

  1. CalitoyStamp Skatepark and game state. Top priorities for Sprint 3. Targeting visible progress by end of next week.

  3. CalitoyMuse product photography. Getting real product shots across all 35 products and finalizing the shop with Printful integration.
